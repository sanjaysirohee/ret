Augmenting Fair price shops with Organized retail outlets

Dr. Manoj Mishra,
Asst. Professor, Chanakya National Law University, Patna., manojmishra@cnlu.ac.in
ORCID ID - 0009-0008-4509-7981
DOI: - _____________________
Keywords: welfare, corporate social responsibility, human needs and desire, inclusion, basic necessities, urban poor.
Abstract:
The idea of welfare for the last man standing is not new. But in this era of corporate social responsibility, we have to ensure that organized retails do not escape from this responsibility. Retailing is always evolving. Thus, we find the concepts keep evolving. And more concepts are just round the corner. This has to do with evolution of the human needs and desire with time. Just emotions do not explain this behaviour. We will find that these items of compromising quality will usually not be bought by the middle and upper middle population of the society. This means that the organized details will have no issues at all carrying the items and dispersing these items. The “PDS plus community retail” is working in some areas where the fair price shops double as outlets for other materials like millets, pulses, dairy products, and daily-use goods in addition to subsidized foodgrains. The problem starts from lack of proper housing in the urban areas slum living conditions in which they stay and the temporary night shelters Are makeshift in nature. Drinking water is another problem that they face due to lack of proper hand pumps and safe water storage units. Livelihood and decent work possibilities also become a challenge at times. Other times these are available. Transport and connectivity are other issues that they have to face because of which the requirements change from the rural poor. In the urban area social protection and documentations are another big challenge as they do not have proper residential addresses and their safety and inclusion are always under a question mark. The climate and disaster resilience in the urban areas is another big challenge that they face and therefore these factors become a priority for the urban poor. The contribution of the organised retail in providing them with their basic necessities will go a long way in fulfilling the needs of the urban poor. These poor people will be at least assured that they will be able to meet the challenges that they face in living in urban areas with minimum earnings. This will also help them to set aside a certain amount of money which they save from these spendings, for the education, and healthcare of their children and themselves. Thus, apart from fulfilling their own needs, the organised retail would be catering to general welfare of the society as well by augmenting fair price shops in their outlet.
